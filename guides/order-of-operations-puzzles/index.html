Order of Operations Puzzles: 6 Questions With Answers

Many quick arithmetic mistakes come from doing a correct operation at the wrong time. These six practice questions make the grouping explicit, so you can check your method without arguing over ambiguous notation.

The useful takeaway

Evaluate brackets and powers first. Multiplication and division share a priority and run left to right; addition and subtraction also share a priority and run left to right.

Use the priority rules, not the letters alone

PEMDAS and BODMAS are reminders for the same basic convention. Neither means that multiplication always comes before division, or that addition always comes before subtraction. Operations in each equal-priority pair are evaluated from left to right.

Write one intermediate line rather than trying to hold the whole expression in memory. For 18 − 3 × 4, first calculate 3 × 4 = 12, then 18 − 12 = 6. Subtracting 3 from 18 first would answer a different, bracketed question.

Questions 1 and 2: the brackets change the answer

1. 8 + 4 × 3
2. (8 + 4) × 3

Calculate both before opening the answer. The same three numbers appear, but they are not grouped in the same way.

Reveal both answers

1: 20. Multiply 4 × 3 = 12, then add 8. 2: 36. Complete the bracket first: 8 + 4 = 12, then multiply by 3. The brackets tell you which total is being multiplied.

When checking a puzzle image, read the symbols as carefully as the numbers. A bracket is part of the question, not decoration.

Questions 3 and 4: equal priority means left to right

3. 24 ÷ 6 × 2
4. 17 − 8 + 3

There are no brackets to change the order. In question 3, division and multiplication share priority. In question 4, subtraction and addition share priority.

Reveal both answers

3: 8. Work left to right: 24 ÷ 6 = 4, then 4 × 2 = 8. Do not turn the expression into 24 ÷ (6 × 2). 4: 12. Calculate 17 − 8 = 9, then 9 + 3 = 12. Adding 8 and 3 first would insert brackets that were not there.

Questions 5 and 6: combine a power with a bracket

5. 3² + 2 × 5
6. 30 ÷ (2 + 3) + 4²

Resolve each bracket or power, then finish the remaining expression using the same rules.

Reveal both answers

5: 19. The square is 9 and the product is 10; 9 + 10 = 19. 6: 22. The bracket is 5 and 4² is 16. You now have 30 ÷ 5 + 16 = 6 + 16 = 22.

Notice that 3² means 3 × 3, not 3 × 2. If powers are unfamiliar, expand them into repeated multiplication on a separate line before continuing.

Keep a useful mistake log

Turn an incorrect answer into a specific check
MistakeWhat to check next time
Added before multiplyingMark multiplication or division before the final addition
Multiplied before a division to its leftTreat multiplication and division as one priority level
Ignored a bracketRewrite the bracket as one intermediate value
Read a square as doublingExpand the power as repeated multiplication

Record the line where your method changed the expression, rather than writing only “wrong.” Then change one number and solve a fresh example. For instance, after correcting 24 ÷ 6 × 2, try 36 ÷ 9 × 3; the answer is 12.

Do not train speed using expressions whose grouping is unclear. Write explicit multiplication symbols and brackets instead of relying on cramped or ambiguous image layouts.

Common questions

Does multiplication always come before division?

No. They have equal priority. Evaluate them from left to right unless brackets specify a different grouping.

Do BODMAS and PEMDAS give different answers?

Not when applied correctly to the same clearly written expression. Both put multiplication/division at one priority level and addition/subtraction at another.

Can I solve these questions without installing the app?

Yes. Every question and explanation on this page is available in your browser. The app offers a separate collection for continued play.
