Reverse 52-Week Savings Challenge: Free Printable Chart & CSV

The reverse challenge starts with $52 and reduces the deposit by $1 each week until the final $1 contribution. Completing every deposit totals $1,378. Use the full chart below or open the free printable checklist to plan the larger opening weeks.

The useful takeaway

Reversing the order changes when you need the money, not how much the deposits add up to. Compare both schedules with your expected expenses.

See how the reverse schedule works

For week n, the reverse deposit is 53 − n dollars. Week one is $52, week two is $51, and week 52 is $1. The standard version uses those same 52 amounts in the opposite order.

If your available money is greater near the beginning of the challenge, the reverse pattern may be easier to maintain later. If money is tight at the start, the opening deposit can be a barrier. Neither order creates a larger deposit total by itself.

Map deposits to your own cash flow

Write down the next several pay dates and the large expenses you already expect. Then compare those dates with the schedule. A calendar-year reverse challenge has smaller deposits near year-end, but someone starting in September will have a different relationship between challenge weeks and seasonal expenses.

For example, the first four reverse deposits are $52, $51, $50 and $49: a total of $202. If that opening block is too large, halving each deposit gives $101 for the same block and $689 for the completed challenge. These numbers describe alternatives; the suitable amount depends on your budget.

Resize the reverse plan or compare another schedule

The full chart and downloadable worksheets use the classic $52-to-$1 amounts. To make a half-size reverse plan, multiply each planned deposit by 0.5: start with $26, finish with $0.50 and total $689 if all 52 deposits are completed. The printable chart can be a reference while you write your adjusted amounts in a separate note or spreadsheet.

The free 52-week savings calculator models a starting deposit with a nonnegative weekly increase. It can compare a flat schedule or show the total of the matching increasing version. For example, a $0.50 start and $0.50 increase also totals $689, but its rows run in the other direction. Use the reverse chart above for the decreasing order; the calculator does not accept a negative weekly increase.

In Savings Goal Tracker, record actual contributions as you make them. This keeps your saved balance accurate whether you follow the reverse order or change the sequence. Entering a contribution does not move money.

Handle a missed week without double-counting

Suppose you complete the first three reverse deposits and have $153. If you miss the next $49 contribution, your balance remains $153. You can keep the $49 as a pending plan item, split it across later dates if affordable, or adjust the goal timeline.

Do not record the missed amount as saved just to preserve a visual streak. The number you need for future planning is the money available for the goal. At review time, subtract that actual balance from your target and decide whether the original schedule still fits.

Common questions

Does the reverse challenge save more?

Its planned deposits total the same $1,378 as the classic $1-to-$52 challenge. The order of deposits changes; interest is not included.

Can I use the calculator for reverse deposits?

Use this guide’s full reverse chart, printable checklist or CSV for the decreasing schedule. The calculator shows flat or increasing plans; it does not accept a negative weekly increase.

Is the reverse savings chart free to print?

Yes. The website’s printable chart and CSV are free and do not require the app or Premium. Open the printable chart and use your browser’s Print command to print or save a PDF. App Premium exports are separate.
