Quit Smoking Checklist: Prepare for Your Quit Date

Choosing a quit date puts an intention on the calendar. This checklist turns it into a few concrete arrangements: who can support you, what your day looks like and what information you want to record.

By MoocsoftSeptember 13, 20264 min read
Open the free quit smoking savings calculator →Explore QuitBit →
The useful takeaway

Separate preparation from progress tracking. Arrange appropriate support before the date, prepare for your actual schedule, and start your current smoke-free record from the moment it begins.

Put your support plan ahead of the counter

Smokefree.gov’s preparation guide recommends choosing a quit date, considering quitting support and telling people how they can help. If you want advice about medication or withdrawal, arrange a conversation with a healthcare professional or pharmacist rather than relying on an app to select treatment.

Write down how you will reach that support. “Ask someone” leaves an unanswered task; a saved service contact and a planned time to call are arrangements you can check. Make sure any service you choose is relevant to where you live.

Use a checklist with a real definition of ready

A practical preparation checklist
ItemWhat ready looks like
Quit dateThe date is written down and I know which day of my schedule it falls on
Professional supportI know whom to contact and have discussed treatment questions if needed
Personal supportA trusted person knows the specific kind of check-in I want
Daily scheduleI have considered my commute, breaks, meals and evening
Progress baselineMy previous daily use, pack size and pack price are recorded
ReviewI have chosen a convenient time to note what needs adjusting

You can copy this into a note and add “ready,” “needs a decision” or “not relevant” beside each row. It is an organizational worksheet, not a test of whether you deserve to begin. An unfinished row tells you what to arrange next.

Walk through the day you will actually have

Imagine a specific weekday or weekend rather than an ideal day with no interruptions. Where will you be after breakfast? Is the afternoon predictable? Will you be with people who smoke? Note the parts of the schedule for which you want a plan, without assuming every hour will be difficult.

For a workday, the smoke-free work-break worksheet helps you fit arrangements around shift times and workplace rules. For an uncertain moment, prepare a small craving action card. Keep these notes short enough to find the relevant line quickly.

A supportive check-in can be specific without becoming surveillance. For example: “Could you message me after my shift and ask how the plan went?” Agree on the arrangement with that person first.

Keep a baseline without pretending the attempt has started

Before the date, record the average cigarettes per day, cigarettes per pack and usual pack price from your previous routine. These are the inputs used to estimate avoided cigarette spending. You can prepare them without starting a smoke-free timer early.

When your smoke-free period begins, use that date and time in the quit smoking savings calculator. It will reject future dates. If you use QuitBit, set up the app’s own progress record separately; the web calculator does not import or synchronize entries with it.

End with a short review, not a scorecard

Choose a convenient moment to write three observations: what was prepared, what was unexpectedly awkward, and what needs to change for tomorrow. An example might be: “My support contact was available; the lunch location was closed; I need another option for that break.”

That review describes your arrangements, not your worth. If you smoked, record what happened accurately and seek the support you need. The setback worksheet can help organize the next entry. If the day went as planned, keep any useful detail for a later personal milestone review.

QuitBit provides a place to track time and history alongside your notes. This checklist and the app are companions to your quit-support plan, not medical guidance or a promise about your outcome.

Common questions

Should I start my smoke-free timer when I decide to quit?

Use the beginning of your actual smoke-free period. Your planning date and quit date can be different; recording both in notes avoids confusing them.

Does completing the checklist guarantee I will quit?

No. It organizes practical preparation. Quitting experiences vary, and professional cessation support can help you consider appropriate treatment and next steps.
