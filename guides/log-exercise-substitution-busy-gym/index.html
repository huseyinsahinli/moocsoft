Busy Gym? How to Log an Exercise Substitution

When you change an exercise because the equipment is unavailable, log the movement you actually completed under its own name and equipment label. Do not copy its results into the original exercise just to make the plan look finished.

The useful takeaway

A substitution needs a new exercise label, a clear weight convention and actual set results. Keep the reason for the change, then compare the record with the same setup—not with the machine you did not use.

The machine is occupied. The log is not broken.

Alex arrives with a planned cable-row exercise. The machine is busy, and there is not enough time to wait. Alex already has a coach-approved alternative for this situation and uses it instead. This is a fictional teaching scene, not a customer story or a recommendation to swap these exercises.

The planned line says “Seated cable row — cable machine A — 10 reps at 30 kg.” The completed work uses a bench-supported dumbbell row with a 12 kg dumbbell. Alex is tempted to tap through the old targets and leave a note saying “machine busy.” That would put dumbbell results under a cable-machine label.

The better record is boring but useful: keep the original work identifiable as not completed, record the replacement by its actual name, and explain why the session changed. You can do that in a paper log first; the principle does not depend on a particular app control.

Write the replacement as its own entry

Illustrative replacement record—not a routine to follow
Record fieldWhat Alex writes
Planned exerciseSeated cable row — cable machine A
Change reasonCable machine unavailable; used the previously agreed alternative
Completed exerciseBench-supported dumbbell row — 12 kg dumbbell
Weight convention12 kg is the weight of one dumbbell, not a pair
Repetition conventionReps are recorded per side; both sides matched in this example
Actual resultsSet 1: 8 per side; set 2: 8 per side; set 3: 7 per side

If the sides differ, write the two results explicitly rather than averaging them. If the setup changes again, note it. “Row, 12, 8” is much harder to interpret a month later than a name, equipment label and stated convention.

The template describes the record you want, not a promise that an app automatically recognizes substitutions, checks exercise suitability or has separate left/right input fields. A short note can carry context that does not have its own field.

Why the new number is not an automatic personal record

The planned 30 kg and the completed 12 kg refer to different equipment and movements. This is not a calculation of how much weaker or stronger Alex became. Even matching numbers on two different machines would not establish an equivalent load or training effect.

Do not combine the old target and replacement results into a single “row progress” line. The next cable-machine session belongs beside a comparable cable-machine session. The next dumbbell session belongs beside the dumbbell record, using the same per-dumbbell and per-side conventions.

A useful note for next time is: “12 kg per dumbbell; 8, 8, 7 per side; bench-supported; replacement because cable machine unavailable.” That is enough to explain what happened without inventing a new personal best. Keep an interruption from becoming a misleading target

Before your next session, ask three small questions:

  1. Which exercise did I actually complete, and with which equipment?
  2. Which result belongs to that setup, and which numbers were only the original plan?
  3. Am I returning to the original plan or intentionally using the alternative again?

Do not let a busy-gym workaround silently rewrite your usual program. Your existing program or qualified coach should decide exercise selection and future targets; a log preserves the facts.

Common questions

Should I enter a substitute exercise under the original name?

Use the movement and equipment you actually used. Keep the reason for the change in a note and do not present the original planned sets as completed.

Can I compare kilograms from a machine and dumbbells directly?

Not as an equivalent-load or personal-record comparison. The numbers describe different setups. Keep each exercise and weight convention identifiable.

Does Did You Lift choose a replacement exercise for me?

This guide does not claim automatic substitutions or individualized coaching. Choose appropriate exercises from your program or qualified coaching, then use the app to organize and record the work.
