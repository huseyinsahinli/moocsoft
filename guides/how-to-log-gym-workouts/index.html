How to Log Gym Workouts: A Set-by-Set Template

The most useful workout log separates what you intended to do from what you completed. A few consistent fields are enough to make the next comparable session easier to understand.

The useful takeaway

Record the exercise, equipment, set number, actual reps or time, and load. Keep planned targets separate and use the same naming and weight conventions each time.

Set up a record you can use between sets

A useful entry has a date, a clear exercise name, the planned target and what you actually completed. Did You Lift combines that set-by-set record with a weekly planner and automatic rest timer on iPhone and Android. The same fields also work in a notebook if you want to try the method first.

Choose a name you can recognize next week

“Row” can describe a cable row, dumbbell row or machine exercise. Pick a name that identifies the movement and record the equipment. If the setup matters to comparison, keep a short note such as the machine setting or the grip used.

Consistency matters more than elaborate naming. Two sessions with the same label should describe similar work. If you deliberately change the exercise or setup, make that change visible in the record.

Separate the target from the completed set

Illustrative logging example, not a training prescription
SetPlanned targetActual resultNote
110 reps at 30 kg10 reps at 30 kgCompleted as planned
210 reps at 30 kg9 reps at 30 kgKeep actual reps in the log
310 reps at 30 kg8 reps at 30 kgDo not copy the target into the result

This example records 27 actual repetitions, not the planned 30. At the same entered load of 30 kg, the completed volume load is:

(10 × 30) + (9 × 30) + (8 × 30) = 810 kg

Copying the target into every result would instead report 900 kg and overstate what the record shows. You can check the arithmetic in the free workout volume calculator. The number summarizes the entered reps and load; it does not measure technique or decide what you should lift next.

If you complete only the first two sets, leave the third as uncompleted and add context if useful. A planned set that did not happen is different from a completed set with zero external weight.

Define your weight and time conventions

For a barbell exercise, be clear whether the entered load includes the bar. For paired dumbbells, choose whether your record describes one dumbbell or the combined external load. Keep that convention consistent and mention it in the exercise note.

Did You Lift uses kilogram targets for strength sets and seconds for timed movements. If your equipment is labeled differently, convert deliberately before entering a target. Do not mix pounds and kilograms under the same label and then compare the numbers directly.

For bodyweight movements without added load, record reps or duration even when weighted volume is zero. The absence of an entered external weight does not make the exercise disappear from your session.

Review the last comparable session before the next one

A short note can explain why two sessions differ. A different machine, a changed order or an interrupted session may be useful context. Before comparing numbers, ask:

  1. Was this the same exercise and equipment setup?
  2. Did both entries use the same units and dumbbell or barbell convention?
  3. Which sets were actually completed, and did reps or load change?
  4. Was there a change in rest, setup or session order worth noting?

For the example above, “30 kg for 10, 9 and 8 reps” is the useful record to carry forward. It does not automatically mean that the next session should use a heavier weight or a different number of sets. Use your existing program or a qualified coach to decide how targets should change.

If your aim is to track changes over time, the progressive-overload logging guide explains how to keep comparisons meaningful.

Common questions

Should I record warm-up sets?

If you record them, label them consistently so you can distinguish them from the working sets you intend to compare. The important part is not silently changing what a total includes.

Should I log planned reps or actual reps?

Keep both if useful, but label them separately. The actual result is the record of what you completed.

What should I record if I stop a workout early?

Keep the results for completed sets, leave remaining planned sets uncompleted, and add a brief note if useful. Do not copy the remaining targets into your actual results.
