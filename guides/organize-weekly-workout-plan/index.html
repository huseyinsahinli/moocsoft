How to Organize a Weekly Workout Plan in Your App

Once you have an appropriate training program, the next job is organization: put the right session on the right day and make every target clear enough to follow in the gym.

By MoocsoftSeptember 12, 20263 min read
Open the free workout volume calculator & rest timer →Explore Did You Lift →
The useful takeaway

Map an existing program to your available days, include rest and other activities, and check each exercise’s targets before starting. A calendar organizes the program; it does not validate it for you.

Start with availability and the full week

Write down the days and time windows you can realistically use. Include work, travel and other activities that affect when you can train. A session that fits on paper but not in your day is difficult to follow consistently.

Strength sessions are only part of a wider activity routine. The CDC adult overview covers both aerobic and muscle-strengthening activity. Use appropriate guidance for your circumstances rather than assuming that a gym calendar alone covers every need.

If you are new to training or have health considerations, get help choosing a suitable program before filling the planner with exercise targets.

This worksheet is an organizational template. It does not specify a training frequency, load or exercise selection. Those decisions should come from a program appropriate to you.

Keep planned sessions and completed sessions distinct

Adding an exercise to Wednesday is a plan. Recording actual reps and weight on Wednesday is a result. If you move the session to another day, keep your history accurate rather than marking the original date completed just because it was scheduled.

The same applies when a session ends early. Record the work you actually did and any relevant note. That gives you a better basis for the next review than a calendar that always appears perfect.

Use consistent exercise names when a movement returns later in the week. A small naming difference can make your record harder to compare even when the actual movement is the same.

Set up the week in Did You Lift

Open a day in the weekly planner, add the exercises and define the target for each set. Use strength sets for reps and kilogram targets or timed sets for duration. Put equipment and setup details in a short exercise note.

Before the session, review the order and rest intervals. When you train, record actual values rather than simply copying targets. Check a shared plan before importing it

A shared Did You Lift plan can include exercise names, equipment, targets, notes and rest settings. Its exported plan does not include the sender’s completed-workout history or actual logged results. A shared target is therefore not evidence of what another person lifted.

Read the preview before accepting an import. In Did You Lift, importing replaces the planned week and ends an active workout; existing completion history remains. Save or export a plan you want to retain using the available app controls before replacing it.

Check every movement and load against your own program and equipment. A technically valid file can still contain a routine that is unsuitable for you.

Common questions

Can I use this worksheet as a complete training program?

No. It organizes a program you already have. It does not prescribe suitable exercises, frequency, intensity or progression.

Does a shared plan include workout history?

Did You Lift exports planned exercises and targets, not completed-workout history or the actual reps and weights logged by the sender.
