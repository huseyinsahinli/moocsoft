Christmas Dinner Timeline for One Oven

Start with the time the food should reach the table. For each dish, write down its oven temperature, how much rack space it needs, and what has to happen after it comes out. Two trays that need the same rack at different temperatures are a scheduling conflict, even if their cooking times look short.

Worked example for a 6 p.m. dinner
TimeOvenWhat happens elsewhere
4:45 p.m.Preheat for the gratin.Set out the serving dishes and their spoons.
5:00–5:45Bake the gratin for the 45 minutes in your recipe.Set the table and prepare the green beans for the stovetop.
5:45–5:50Take out the gratin and check the oven setting for the rolls.Let the gratin rest as directed. A helper fills the water glasses.
5:50–6:00Warm the rolls if the oven is at the temperature your recipe calls for.Finish the beans, dress the salad and carry the dishes to the table.

What fits in the oven at the same time?

Try the empty pans on the actual racks before the day. Include handles and lids. In this example the gratin occupies the main rack, the beans cook on the stove, and the salad needs no heat. The schedule assumes your gratin recipe allows a 15-minute rest and your rolls need 10 minutes. Replace both with the timings for your dishes.

What if two dishes need the same slot?

Suppose a guest offers a second baked side that needs the oven from 5:30 until 6. The gratin is still in there. Ask whether the guest would like to bring a cold side instead, or serve the rolls at room temperature and give the last slot to a dish that fits it. Decide before shopping, while the menu is still easy to change.

Give each helper a handoff they can see

“When the gratin comes out, put the water glasses on the table” is easier to follow than “help around six.” Write one name beside each last-minute job. Our Christmas Dinner Planner has separate oven and serving pages, so the person setting the table can take their sheet out of the kitchen.
