Christmas Mystery Game for Kids: A Host’s Guide

Give each team a pencil, a place to spread out its clues and a grid everyone can see. Keep the answer key with the host. Before opening the case, try the little practice puzzle below so younger detectives get a turn at reasoning before the faster readers race ahead.

A two-clue warm-up, separate from the printable game
Read aloudAsk the team
A bell is in one of three boxes: red, blue or green.What are the three possibilities?
Clue one: The green box is empty.Which box can you cross out?
Clue two: The red box holds only a ribbon.Which box must hold the bell, and why?

Let a child explain the first deduction

The bell must be in the blue box. Ask someone to explain why both other boxes are ruled out. If they guessed blue straight away, invite another player to point to the clue that rules out red. This gives the group a shared rule for the main game: every mark on the grid needs a reason.

Divide the jobs around the table

In our printable case, The Missing Tree Star, teams have six witness clues, two logic grids and a letter code to work through. One player reads a statement, another marks the grid, and a third asks which words support the mark. Swap jobs after each clue. A child who prefers listening can point to the square while a partner writes.

Offer a hint without giving away the ending

If the group stalls, ask them to show you their last certain mark. Then ask which witness helped them make it. Often two players have understood a word differently. Let them reread the line together before offering a printed hint. Give the team the letter-code worksheet when they reach it. Keep your answer key face down until everyone has had a chance to explain a theory.
