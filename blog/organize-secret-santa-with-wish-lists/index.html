Secret Santa Questionnaire Ideas for Work and Family

The most useful Secret Santa question is one a giver can shop from. “What do you reach for on a rainy afternoon?” might get you “crosswords and ginger tea.” That gives the giver two affordable ideas and leaves the surprise intact.

Three prompts that give a shopper a starting point

Ask “What small treat do you buy for yourself?”, “What hobby could always use fresh supplies?” and “Is there anything you’d prefer to skip?” A reply like “dark chocolate, black sketch pens, and please skip scented candles” gives the shopper room to choose. Share the budget before people fill in their forms.

Adjust the questions for an office or a family

For an office draw, make it easy to opt in privately and keep the prompts about everyday tastes. For family, try one question about a shared memory. A cousin who remembers making popcorn on movie nights has a gift idea even if the recipient leaves the rest blank.

Keep the matches private through gift day

Collect forms, draw names and check the organizer sheet for self-matches or anyone assigned twice. Send each giver their recipient’s form. Put the exchange date and drop-off location in the same message. On the day, label packages with the recipient’s name so the organizer can spot a missing gift before people leave.
